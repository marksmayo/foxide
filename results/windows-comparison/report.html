The rules behind the medals

Reproduce the result, inspect the limits, and rerun the comparison as Foxide grows.

Fair comparisons need explicit boundaries.

  1. 01
    Matched inputs, independent launches. The same SHA-256-identified synthetic media is used for every entrant. New processes, warm-cache priming, seeded shuffled player order, and one player at a time.
  2. 02
    Playback evidence before ranking. Engine diagnostics or advancing runtime playback clocks are required. A process waiting in a setup screen cannot win a low-RAM medal. Engine evidence still does not prove screen presentation.
  3. 03
    At least three complete, comparable trials. Medals use the median of run summaries. Missing values stay missing; one-player categories receive no gold. Ties at displayed precision share a placement; vacant medal slots stay vacant.
  4. 04
    Resource cost is not quality parity. Default hardware decode, scaling, audio route and rendering differ. No complete display-drop census, HDR visual validation or controlled background workload was established. Lower CPU can coexist with dropped frames.
  5. 05
    No invented battery or startup victory. Window readiness, internal engine events, CPU percentage and GPU allocation cannot substitute for displayed TTFF, seek presentation, Watts or battery runtime.
